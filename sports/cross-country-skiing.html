Cross-Country Skiing

Marathon running on snow. Possibly the toughest endurance sport.

Competitors
Individual, plus relays and team sprints
Race length
Sprint about 3 min, 50 km about 2 hours
Season
November to March
Olympic events
Sprint, distance races, skiathlon, relays

What is it?

Cross-country skiers race across rolling, snowy terrain using their own power: legs, arms and poles. Races range from short sprints to grueling 50 km marathons. Elite skiers have some of the highest fitness levels ever measured.

Norway is completely obsessed with it. Hundreds of thousands of people line the trails there, and the sport's stars are national heroes.

The basic rules

  1. There are two techniques: classic (skis parallel in grooved tracks) and skate (pushing off sideways like an ice skater). Each race specifies which.
  2. In mass start races, everyone starts together and the first across the line wins.
  3. In interval start races, skiers start 30 seconds apart and race the clock.
  4. Sprints use knockout heats of six skiers each.
  5. The skiathlon starts in classic, then skiers switch skis and finish in skate.

  • Therese JohaugNorwegian distance star.
  • Jessie DigginsAmerican who won the historic 2018 team sprint gold.

Lingo you'll hear

Classic
Skis parallel in tracks.
Skate
The faster, side-to-side technique.
Wax
Applied to skis for grip and glide. A crucial secret science.
Double poling
Pushing with both poles at once.

💬 Sound smart at a party

“The wax techs decided that race.” The right ski wax can be worth minutes.
