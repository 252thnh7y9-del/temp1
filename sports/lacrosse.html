Lacrosse

North America's oldest sport: a stick with a net and a very fast ball.

Players
Men's field: 10 per team. Women's (NCAA): 12.
Game length
4 quarters × 15 min
Season
College: February to May
Big final
The NCAA championship on Memorial Day weekend

What is it?

Lacrosse was created by Indigenous peoples of North America, especially the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois), centuries ago. It's often called “the Creator's game.” Players use sticks with a net pocket to catch, carry, pass and shoot a hard rubber ball into the goal.

It's a big college sport in the US Northeast and Mid-Atlantic, and Canada's national summer sport. It returns to the Olympics at Los Angeles 2028 in a fast six-a-side format.

The basic rules

  1. Score by shooting the ball into the 6×6 ft goal: 1 point. The pro PLL also has 2-point shots from long range.
  2. Players carry the ball by cradling it in the stick's pocket. Only the goalie can touch it with their hands.
  3. Each quarter and each goal restarts with a faceoff: two players fight for the ball at midfield.
  4. Men's lacrosse allows body checks and stick checks, so players wear helmets and pads. Women's lacrosse limits contact.
  5. Nobody but the defenders and goalie can enter the crease, the circle around the goal.
  6. Fouls send players to the penalty box, giving the other team a man-up advantage.

Major leagues & competitions

  • NCAA lacrosseThe heart of the sport in the US.
  • Premier Lacrosse League (PLL)The top pro outdoor league.
  • National Lacrosse League (NLL)Indoor “box” lacrosse, popular in Canada.
  • World Lacrosse ChampionshipNational teams, every four years.

Famous teams

  • Johns Hopkins Blue JaysLacrosse is Hopkins' marquee sport.
  • Maryland TerrapinsThe dominant women's program, and strong men's too.
  • Northwestern WildcatsA women's dynasty in the 2000s.

Famous names to know

  • Jim BrownSyracuse lacrosse legend, and NFL Hall of Famer.
