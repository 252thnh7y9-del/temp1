Hurling (Gaelic Games)

Ireland's ancient game, often called the fastest field sport in the world.

Players
15 per team
Game length
2 halves × 35 min
Season
Spring to summer
Big final
The All-Ireland Final at Croke Park, Dublin

What is it?

Hurling is an Irish sport said to be more than 3,000 years old. Two teams of 15 use wooden sticks called hurleys to hit a small leather ball, the sliotar, at speeds of over 150 km/h. Players can catch it, balance it on the stick while running and strike it the length of the field.

The players are amateurs, unpaid, and play for their home county. That gives the sport a fiercely local passion. Its sister sport, Gaelic football, is the most popular sport in Ireland. Camogie is the women's version of hurling.

The basic rules

  1. The goals look like rugby posts with a soccer net below the crossbar.
  2. Ball over the crossbar: 1 point. Ball into the net: a goal, worth 3 points.
  3. Scores are written like this: 2–15 means 2 goals and 15 points, for 21 in total.
  4. You can catch the ball, but can carry it in your hand for only four steps. Then you must balance or bounce it on the hurley, or play it.
  5. Some shoulder-to-shoulder contact is allowed. Helmets have been compulsory since 2010.

Major leagues & competitions

  • All-Ireland Senior Hurling ChampionshipThe biggest competition, with counties competing through spring and summer.
  • National Hurling LeagueA spring league.
  • GAAThe Gaelic Athletic Association, an amateur organization at the heart of Irish communities.

Famous teams

  • Kilkenny“The Cats,” with the most All-Ireland titles.
  • Cork“The Rebels,” a historic powerhouse.
  • TipperaryOne of the “big three.”
  • LimerickThe dominant team of the early 2020s, with four titles in a row (2020–2023).

Famous names to know

  • Henry ShefflinKilkenny legend who won a record 10 All-Ireland medals on the field.
  • Christy RingCork icon of the 1940s and 1950s.
  • DJ CareyKilkenny's dazzling star of the 1990s.
  • TJ ReidKilkenny's modern scoring machine.

Lingo you'll hear

Sliotar
The ball (say “slit-er”).
Hurley / camán
The stick.
Puck-out
The goalkeeper restarting play with a long strike.
Sideline cut
A difficult shot from the sideline.

💬 Sound smart at a party

“They're only amateurs, you know.” Everyone is amazed that they're unpaid.
