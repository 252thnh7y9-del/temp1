Skeleton

Head-first down an ice track at 130 km/h, chin inches from the ice.

Competitors
Individual, plus a mixed team event
Run length
About 50–60 seconds
Season
November to February
Olympic events
Men's, women's and mixed team

What is it?

Skeleton athletes sprint, dive head-first onto a small sled and slide face-down down the same icy track used for bobsled and luge. Their chins are just inches above the ice at speeds over 130 km/h (80 mph).

They steer with tiny shifts of the shoulders and knees. It's not as dangerous as it looks, but it certainly looks terrifying.

The basic rules

  1. Athletes make 4 runs over two days. The lowest combined time wins.
  2. The sprint start, pushing the sled before diving on, is crucial.
  3. No steering or braking devices: athletes steer with their body.
  4. Athletes wear helmets with chin guards and spiked shoes.
  5. A mixed team event joined the Olympics in 2026.
