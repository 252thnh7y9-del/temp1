Freestyle Skiing

Flips, bumps and big air. Skiing's acrobatic side.

Competitors
Individual (plus a mixed team aerials event)
Format
Judged runs, or head-to-head racing in ski cross
Season
December to March
Olympic events
Moguls, aerials, ski cross, halfpipe, slopestyle, big air

What is it?

Freestyle skiing is the creative, acrobatic branch of skiing. Some events are judged on tricks: flips and spins off huge jumps, or runs through a halfpipe. Others are races, like ski cross, where four skiers race down a course of jumps and banked turns.

It grew out of the 1960s and 1970s “hot dog” skiing scene and is a favorite with younger fans.

The basic rules

  1. Moguls: ski down a bumpy course with two jumps. Judges score turns and jumps, and speed also counts.
  2. Aerials: launch off a steep kicker and perform flips and twists. Scored on takeoff, air and landing, multiplied by difficulty.
  3. Halfpipe, slopestyle, big air: judges score the difficulty, height, style and landing of tricks.
  4. Ski cross: four skiers race at once, and the top two advance from each heat.
  5. In most judged events, each athlete's best run counts.

Major leagues & competitions

  • SwitzerlandStrong all-around.

  • Mikaël KingsburyCanadian moguls GOAT.
  • David WiseAmerican two-time halfpipe Olympic champion.
  • Jonny MoseleyAmerican moguls champion of 1998.

Lingo you'll hear

Kicker
A jump ramp.
Grab
Holding the skis during a trick.
Switch
Skiing or landing backward.
Double cork
An off-axis double flip.

💬 Sound smart at a party

“That grab was really stylish.” Style counts in judged freestyle events.
