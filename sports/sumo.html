Sumo Wrestling

Japan's ancient sport: giants, rituals and bouts that last seconds.

Competitors
1 vs 1
Bout length
Usually a few seconds, rarely more than a minute
Season
6 tournaments a year, each 15 days long
Top rank
Yokozuna (grand champion)

What is it?

Sumo is Japan's national sport, with roots going back more than 1,500 years. Two wrestlers, called rikishi, face off in a raised clay ring, the dohyō. The goal is simple: force your opponent out of the ring, or make them touch the ground with anything other than the soles of their feet.

Most bouts are over in under ten seconds, but they're wrapped in Shinto ritual: throwing salt to purify the ring, stomping and long stare-downs. Wrestlers live and train together in “stables” under a strict, traditional lifestyle. There are no weight classes, so a smaller, quicker wrestler can beat a much heavier one.

The basic rules

  1. You lose if you step or fall out of the ring, or if any part of your body other than your feet touches the ground.
  2. There are no weight classes. Everyone in a division can face anyone else.
  3. Pushing, slapping, tripping and throwing are allowed. Punching with a closed fist and pulling hair are not.
  4. Wrestlers grip each other's mawashi (the thick belt) to lift, pull and throw.
  5. In a tournament, each top-division wrestler fights once a day for 15 days. The best record wins the title.
  6. Rankings go up or down after each tournament based on results. Winning more bouts than you lose (8 or more of 15) is the key target.

Major leagues & competitions

  • Grand Sumo tournaments (honbasho)Six per year: three in Tokyo, plus Osaka, Nagoya and Fukuoka.
  • Japan Sumo AssociationRuns professional sumo.
  • MakuuchiThe top division, the one shown on TV.

Where to see it

  • Ryōgoku KokugikanTokyo's sumo hall, which hosts three tournaments a year.
  • Stables (heya)Wrestlers belong to stables, and some let visitors watch morning practice.
  • NHK broadcastsJapan's public broadcaster shows the daily bouts, with English commentary online.

Famous names to know

  • HakuhōMongolian-born yokozuna with a record 45 top-division titles.
  • Taihō1960s legend with 32 titles, a national hero in Japan.
  • Chiyonofuji“The Wolf,” a famously muscular yokozuna of the 1980s.
  • AkebonoFrom Hawaii, the first foreign-born yokozuna (1993).
  • ŌnosatoA fast-rising star who reached yokozuna in 2025.

Lingo you'll hear

Rikishi
A sumo wrestler.
Yokozuna
Grand champion, the highest rank. Very few ever reach it.
Banzuke
The official ranking list, written in calligraphy.
Kinboshi
A “gold star”: when a low-ranked wrestler beats a yokozuna. Fans throw cushions.

💬 Sound smart at a party

“The tachi-ai decides everything.” The initial charge usually wins the bout.
