Snowboarding

Big tricks, big air and a laid-back culture.

Competitors
Individual, plus a mixed team cross event
Format
Judged runs, or head-to-head races
Season
December to March
Olympic events
Halfpipe, slopestyle, big air, cross, parallel giant slalom

What is it?

Snowboarding grew out of surf and skate culture and joined the Olympics in 1998. Riders perform tricks in judged events like halfpipe, slopestyle and big air, or race in snowboard cross and parallel giant slalom.

Its stars, especially in the halfpipe, are some of the biggest names of the Winter Olympics.

The basic rules

  1. Halfpipe: ride a U-shaped wall and perform tricks above the lip. Judges score difficulty, amplitude and execution.
  2. Slopestyle: a course of rails and jumps. Big air: one giant jump, one trick.
  3. In judged events, riders usually get several runs and the best counts.
  4. Snowboard cross: four to six riders race down a course of jumps and turns.
  5. Parallel giant slalom: two riders race side by side.
