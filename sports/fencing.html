Fencing

Sword fighting at lightning speed, scored electronically.

Competitors
1 vs 1, plus teams
Bout length
To 15 touches, or three 3-minute periods
Weapons
Foil, épée, sabre
Biggest events
The Olympics and the World Championships

What is it?

Fencing is modern sword fighting. Two fencers face off on a long, narrow strip called a piste and score by touching their opponent with their blade. Electronic sensors register hits that happen faster than the eye can follow.

There are three weapons: foil, épée and sabre, each with its own rules. Fencing has been in every modern Olympics since 1896.

The basic rules

  1. Foil: hit with the tip, only on the torso.
  2. Épée: hit with the tip, anywhere on the body. Both fencers can score at once.
  3. Sabre: hit with the edge or tip, anywhere above the waist.
  4. In foil and sabre, right of way decides who scores when both hit: whoever attacked first.
  5. Elimination bouts go to 15 touches.
