Biathlon

Cross-country skiing plus rifle shooting. Hearts racing, hands steady.

Competitors
Individual, plus relays
Target distance
50 meters
Season
November to March (World Cup)
Olympic events
Sprint, pursuit, individual, mass start, relays

What is it?

Biathlon combines two opposite skills: exhausting cross-country ski racing and precise rifle shooting. Athletes ski a loop, stop at the range, try to calm their pounding heart and hit five small targets, then ski off again.

Every miss costs time, so races swing wildly at the shooting range. It's one of the most-watched winter sports in Germany, Norway and France.

The basic rules

  1. Athletes ski with a rifle on their back, using the fast “skating” technique.
  2. At each shooting stage, they fire 5 shots at 5 targets, either lying down (prone) or standing.
  3. Each miss usually means skiing a 150-meter penalty loop. In the individual race, it's a 1-minute time penalty instead.
  4. In most races, the first across the finish line wins. Some events are time trials.
  5. In the pursuit, athletes start in the order and time gaps from a previous race.

Famous names to know

  • Ole Einar BjørndalenNorwegian “King of Biathlon,” with 13 Olympic medals.
  • Martin FourcadeFrench five-time Olympic champion.
  • Johannes Thingnes BøNorwegian who dominated the 2020s.
  • Magdalena NeunerGerman star who retired young as a national hero.

Lingo you'll hear

Clean
Hitting all five targets.
Prone
Shooting while lying down.
Penalty loop
The extra lap for each miss.
Range time
How long an athlete spends shooting.

💬 Sound smart at a party

“It's won or lost on the range.” Shooting usually decides the race.
