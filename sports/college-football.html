College Football

Saturdays in the fall: rivalries, marching bands and 100,000-seat stadiums.

Players on the field
11 per team
Season
Late August to January
Championship
A 12-team College Football Playoff
Top award
The Heisman Trophy

What is it?

College football is American football played by university teams, and in much of the US it's even bigger than the NFL. Games are on Saturdays in the fall, with tailgates, marching bands, mascots and student sections that make it feel like a festival.

Rules are close to the NFL's, but the culture is different: century-old rivalries, deep local loyalty and stadiums seating over 100,000 fans. Players are students. Since 2021 they can earn money from endorsements (NIL), and since 2025 schools can pay them directly.

The basic rules

  1. Scoring and downs are the same as in the NFL. See our American football page.
  2. A catch needs only one foot in bounds, compared with two in the NFL.
  3. In overtime, each team gets the ball near the opponent's end zone, taking turns until one team leads after a round.
  4. Teams belong to conferences (like the SEC and Big Ten) and play most games against conference rivals.
  5. After the season, the top teams enter the College Football Playoff. Many others play in traditional bowl games.

Major leagues & competitions

  • SEC (Southeastern Conference)The South's powerhouse conference, “It just means more.”
  • Big TenMidwest and coast-to-coast giants like Ohio State, Michigan and USC.
  • Big 12 / ACCThe other two of the “Power Four” conferences.
  • College Football PlayoffDecides the national champion.
  • Bowl gamesIncluding the Rose Bowl, “the Granddaddy of Them All.”

Famous programs & rivalries

  • Alabama Crimson TideA dynasty under coach Nick Saban.
  • Ohio State vs Michigan“The Game,” perhaps the greatest rivalry in American sports.
  • Notre Dame Fighting IrishIndependent of any conference, with a national following.
  • Texas LonghornsA huge program with an enormous budget.
  • Georgia BulldogsBack-to-back national champions in the 2021 and 2022 seasons.
  • Army vs NavyA historic service-academy rivalry, attended by the President.

Famous names to know

  • Archie GriffinOhio State. The only two-time Heisman winner.
  • Tim TebowFlorida quarterback and cultural phenomenon.
  • Bo JacksonAuburn's Heisman winner who also became a pro baseball star.
  • Nick SabanCoach with a record 7 national titles (6 at Alabama).
  • Doug FlutieBoston College, known for the famous 1984 “Hail Flutie” pass.

Lingo you'll hear

Tailgate
The pre-game parking-lot party.
Redshirt
Sitting out a year to save a year of eligibility.
Walk-on
A player who joins without a scholarship.
Transfer portal
How players switch schools, now very common.

💬 Sound smart at a party

“It's all about recruiting.” The top programs win by signing the best high schoolers.
