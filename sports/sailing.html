Sailing

Wind, tactics and speed, now with flying hydrofoils.

Competitors
Solo, two-person and mixed crews
Boats
Dinghies, skiffs, catamarans, windsurfers, kites
Scoring
Low-point system
Biggest events
The Olympics and the America's Cup

What is it?

Olympic sailing races small boats, windsurfers and kiteboards around marked courses. Sailors read the wind and water, plan their tactics and maneuver around rivals.

Many modern boats use foils, underwater wings that lift the hull out of the water so it flies above the surface at high speed.

The basic rules

  1. Boats race around a course of marks (buoys) in a fleet race.
  2. Points equal your finishing position: 1st gets 1 point. Lowest total wins.
  3. The final medal race counts for double points.
  4. Right-of-way rules, like starboard over port, prevent collisions.
  5. Breaking a rule can mean a penalty turn or disqualification.
