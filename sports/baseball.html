Baseball

America's pastime: a summer game of pitcher versus batter.

Players on the field
9 fielders for the defense
Game length
9 innings (about 2.5–3 hours)
Season
Late March to October (162 games)
Big final
The World Series

What is it?

Baseball is a duel at its core: a pitcher throws, a batter tries to hit, and the rest of the game flows from there. Teams take turns batting and fielding. A run scores when a batter makes it all the way around four bases.

There is no game clock, though a pitch clock added in 2023 sped things up. With 162 games a season, baseball is part of everyday summer life: a radio broadcast, a hot dog at the ballpark, a box score in the paper.

The basic rules

  1. An inning has two halves: each team bats once. A half ends when the batting team gets 3 outs.
  2. The pitcher throws toward the batter. A swing-and-miss, or a good pitch that isn't swung at, is a strike. 3 strikes is a strikeout.
  3. A pitch outside the strike zone that isn't swung at is a ball. 4 balls is a walk, and the batter goes to first base free.
  4. Hit the ball and run. You're out if a fielder catches it in the air, or throws it to the base before you get there.
  5. Touching first, second, third and home base scores a run. Hitting it over the outfield wall is a home run.
  6. After 9 innings, the team with more runs wins. Ties go to extra innings.

Major leagues & competitions

  • MLBMajor League Baseball. 30 teams split into the American and National Leagues.
  • Nippon Professional BaseballJapan's top league, with famously passionate fans.
  • KBO LeagueSouth Korea's top league, known for bat flips and cheer squads.
  • World Baseball ClassicAn international tournament between national teams.

Famous teams

  • New York Yankees27 World Series titles, by far the most.
  • Boston Red SoxThe Yankees' archrivals. Ended an 86-year title drought in 2004.
  • Los Angeles DodgersBig-spending powerhouse, and the team of Jackie Robinson (when in Brooklyn).
  • Chicago CubsEnded a 108-year championship drought in 2016.
  • St. Louis CardinalsThe most successful National League franchise.

Famous names to know

  • Babe RuthThe original sports superstar. Home-run-hitting legend of the 1920s.
  • Jackie RobinsonBroke MLB's color barrier in 1947. Every player wears his #42 on April 15.
  • Shohei OhtaniJapanese phenom who both pitches and hits at an elite level.
  • Aaron JudgeYankees slugger who holds the American League home run record.
  • Derek JeterBeloved Yankees shortstop and five-time champion.

Lingo you'll hear

Grand slam
A home run with the bases loaded: 4 runs at once.
Bullpen
The relief pitchers, and the area where they warm up.
No-hitter
A game where a pitcher allows zero hits. Rare and celebrated.
Seventh-inning stretch
When everyone stands and sings “Take Me Out to the Ball Game.”

💬 Sound smart at a party

“Pitching wins championships.” It's a cliché, but nobody will argue with you.
