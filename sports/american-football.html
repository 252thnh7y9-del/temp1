American Football

Chess with collisions, played a few yards at a time.

Players on the field
11 per team
Game length
4 quarters × 15 min (about 3 hours in real time)
Season
September to February
Big final
The Super Bowl

What is it?

American football is a game of territory. Two teams of 11 take turns trying to move an oval ball down a 100-yard field and into the other team's end zone. The game stops after almost every play, which leaves time for strategy (and for snacks).

It is the most-watched sport in the United States. The NFL's championship game, the Super Bowl, is held every February and is as much a cultural event as a game, famous for its halftime show and its ads.

The basic rules

  1. The team with the ball (the offense) gets four tries, called downs, to move the ball 10 yards. If they make it, they get a fresh set of four downs.
  2. If they fail, the other team gets the ball. That's why teams usually kick it away (a punt) on 4th down.
  3. You move the ball by running with it or by throwing a forward pass. The quarterback is the player who usually throws.
  4. Touchdown (carry or catch the ball in the end zone): 6 points, then a 1-point kick or a 2-point play.
  5. Field goal (kick the ball through the tall goalposts): 3 points. Safety (tackling the offense in its own end zone): 2 points.
  6. Yellow flags thrown by referees mean a penalty, usually costing the guilty team some yards.

Major leagues & competitions

  • NFLNational Football League. 32 teams, the top professional league.
  • College football (NCAA)Hugely popular, especially in the American South and Midwest.
  • CFLCanadian Football League. Slightly different rules and a bigger field.

Famous teams

  • Dallas CowboysNicknamed “America's Team” and among the most valuable sports franchises in the world.
  • New England PatriotsWon six Super Bowls in the Tom Brady era.
  • Pittsburgh SteelersSix Super Bowl wins and a famously loyal fan base.
  • Green Bay PackersOwned by their fans and based in a small Wisconsin city.
  • Kansas City ChiefsThe dominant team of the 2020s.
  • San Francisco 49ersA dynasty in the 1980s.

Famous names to know

  • Tom BradyQuarterback with 7 Super Bowl wins, widely seen as the greatest ever.
  • Patrick MahomesChiefs quarterback known for no-look and sidearm throws.
  • Travis KelceChiefs tight end, also famous outside sports for his relationship with Taylor Swift.
  • Jerry RiceHolds nearly every career receiving record.
  • Peyton ManningQuarterback, two Super Bowl wins, now a TV personality.

Lingo you'll hear

Snap
The hike of the ball that starts each play.
Interception
A pass caught by the defense. Bad news for the quarterback.
Sack
Tackling the quarterback before he can throw.
Hail Mary
A long, desperate pass at the end of a half.

💬 Sound smart at a party

“They should go for it on fourth down.” Fans argue about this constantly, and analytics usually back you up.
