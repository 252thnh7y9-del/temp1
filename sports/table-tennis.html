Table Tennis

Not just basement ping-pong: blistering spin at elite level.

Players
1 vs 1 or 2 vs 2
Match length
Best of 5 or 7 games
Season
Year-round
Biggest events
The Olympics and the World Championships

What is it?

Table tennis is played with small paddles and a light plastic ball on a table divided by a low net. At the top level, the ball travels so fast and with so much spin that rallies look superhuman.

It's enormously popular in China, where it is often called the national sport, and also in Japan, Korea and Europe. It has been an Olympic sport since 1988.

The basic rules

  1. Games go to 11 points, and you must win by 2.
  2. Every rally scores a point. Serve switches every 2 points (every point at 10–10).
  3. On a serve, the ball must bounce once on your side, then once on the opponent's side.
  4. In a rally, hit the ball over the net so it bounces on the other side, without letting it bounce twice on yours.
  5. The serving hand must be open and flat, and the ball tossed up at least 16 cm. No hidden spin tricks.

Powerhouse nations

  • ChinaHas won the vast majority of Olympic and world titles since the 1980s.
  • JapanChina's strongest modern rival.
  • GermanyEurope's leading nation.
  • SwedenDominant in the late 1980s and 1990s.

Famous names to know

  • Ma LongChinese, considered the greatest of all time, with multiple Olympic golds.
  • Jan-Ove WaldnerSwedish “Mozart of table tennis.”
  • Deng YapingChinese champion of the 1990s, despite being under 5 feet tall.
  • Fan ZhendongChinese 2024 Olympic singles champion.
  • Sun YingshaChinese women's world No. 1.
  • Timo BollGerman veteran, adored across Europe.

Lingo you'll hear

Loop
A heavy topspin stroke.
Chop
A defensive backspin stroke.
Let
Replay the serve (for example, when it clips the net).
Pips
Bumpy rubber that makes spin unpredictable.

💬 Sound smart at a party

“It's all about the serve and the third ball.” Elite players plan the serve and their next shot together.
