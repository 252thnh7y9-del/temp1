Bobsleigh

A sprint start, then a 150 km/h ride down an ice tube.

Crew
1 (monobob), 2 or 4
Run length
About 50–60 seconds
Season
November to March
Olympic events
Two-man, four-man, two-woman, women's monobob

What is it?

In bobsleigh (or bobsled), teams of two or four sprint while pushing a heavy sled, jump in, and race down a twisting, iced track. The pilot steers, and the brakeman at the back pulls the brake after the finish.

Sleds top 150 km/h (90 mph), and runs are decided by hundredths of a second. The sport became world-famous thanks to Jamaica's 1988 team, the inspiration for the movie Cool Runnings.

The basic rules

  1. Each team makes 4 runs, usually over two days. The lowest combined time wins.
  2. The start matters enormously: athletes push the sled about 50 meters before jumping in.
  3. The pilot steers with ropes connected to the front runners.
  4. Sleds plus crew have minimum and maximum weights, to keep things fair.
  5. Crashing usually ends a team's chances.

Lingo you'll hear

Push
The running start.
Pilot
The driver.
Monobob
A one-woman bobsled event added in 2022.
Skid
Losing grip in a curve, which costs time.
