Ski Jumping

Ski down a ramp, then fly the length of a football field.

Competitors
Individual, plus team events
Jump length
About 100–140 m on Olympic hills
Season
November to March
Olympic events
Normal hill, large hill, team events

What is it?

Ski jumpers speed down a steep ramp at about 90 km/h (55 mph), launch into the air and glide in a V-shape for over 100 meters before landing. It's the closest thing to human flight in sport.

It's hugely popular in Norway, Austria, Germany, Poland, Slovenia and Japan, where big events draw tens of thousands of fans.

The basic rules

  1. Each jump scores distance points plus style points from 5 judges. The highest and lowest judges' scores are dropped.
  2. Landing in the telemark position (one foot ahead of the other, like a lunge) earns better style scores.
  3. Points are adjusted for wind and for the starting gate used.
  4. Competitions usually have two rounds, and the totals decide the winner.
  5. Falls get very low style scores.

  • Simon AmmannSwiss winner of 4 individual Olympic golds.
  • Kamil StochPolish three-time Olympic champion.
  • Sara TakanashiJapanese with the most World Cup wins of any jumper.
  • Eddie “the Eagle” EdwardsBritish underdog who finished last in 1988 and became a folk hero.

Lingo you'll hear

Telemark
The lunging landing position.
V-style
Holding the skis in a V shape in flight.
Inrun
The ramp jumpers ski down.
Hill size
The distance that defines the size of the hill.

💬 Sound smart at a party

“The wind really helped that jump.” Conditions matter so much that scores are adjusted for wind.
