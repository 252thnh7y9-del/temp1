College Basketball (March Madness)

68 teams, three weeks, endless upsets, and everyone fills out a bracket.

Players on the court
5 per team
Game length
Men: 2 halves × 20 min. Women: 4 quarters × 10 min.
Season
November to early April
Big finish
The Final Four

What is it?

College basketball is played by university teams across the US, but its claim to fame is March Madness, the NCAA tournament held each spring. Single elimination means one bad night ends your season, so underdogs regularly knock off giants.

Millions of people who never watch basketball fill out a bracket, predicting every game, for office pools. The odds of a perfect bracket are about 1 in 9.2 quintillion, and nobody has ever verifiably done it.

The basic rules

  1. Basic rules are the same as in the pros. See our basketball page.
  2. The shot clock is 30 seconds, compared with 24 in the NBA.
  3. The NCAA tournament has 68 teams, men's and women's. Lose once and you're out.
  4. Rounds: First Four → Round of 64 → Round of 32 → Sweet 16 → Elite Eight → Final Four → Championship.
  5. Teams are seeded 1 to 16 in four regions. A 16 beating a 1 is the ultimate upset, and it has happened only twice in the men's tournament.

Famous programs

  • UCLA Bruins11 men's titles, 10 of them in 12 years under coach John Wooden.
  • Kentucky WildcatsA storied program with fanatical fans.
  • Duke Blue DevilsA powerhouse built by Coach K. Loved and hated.
  • North Carolina Tar HeelsMichael Jordan's college team, and Duke's archrival.
  • UConn HuskiesA powerhouse in both men's and women's basketball. The women hold the record for most titles.

Famous names to know

  • Christian LaettnerDuke, with the famous buzzer-beater against Kentucky in 1992.
  • Lew Alcindor (Kareem Abdul-Jabbar)Won three straight titles at UCLA.
  • Paige BueckersUConn star who won a title in 2025.
  • Mike Krzyzewski (“Coach K”)Duke coach with the most wins in men's college history.
