Judo

The “gentle way”: throw your opponent flat on their back.

Competitors
1 vs 1 in weight classes, plus a mixed team event
Match length
4 minutes, plus “golden score” overtime
Season
Year-round
Biggest events
The Olympics and the World Championships

What is it?

Judo is a Japanese martial art founded in 1882. Two athletes in heavy jackets (judogi) grip each other and try to throw their opponent onto their back, pin them down, or force a submission. Punches and kicks aren't allowed.

It's hugely popular in Japan and France, which has more registered judoka than any other country.

The basic rules

  1. A clean, forceful throw onto the back scores ippon and wins the match instantly.
  2. A partial throw scores waza-ari. Two waza-ari equal ippon.
  3. Pinning an opponent for 20 seconds is also ippon (10 seconds is waza-ari).
  4. Armlocks and chokes can force a submission.
  5. Passivity or illegal grips earn penalties (shido). Three shidos lose the match.
