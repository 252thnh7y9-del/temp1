Boxing

The sweet science: two fighters, gloves and a lot of strategy.

Competitors
1 vs 1, in weight classes
Fight length
Pro title fights: up to 12 rounds of 3 minutes
Season
Year-round, fight by fight
Ultimate goal
Becoming “undisputed” champion

What is it?

Boxing is a combat sport in which two fighters wearing padded gloves throw punches at each other in a square ring. It's split into weight classes, so fighters face opponents of similar size.

Beyond the punching, boxing is about footwork, defense and outthinking your opponent. That's why it's nicknamed “the sweet science.” Big fights are huge pay-per-view events, and boxing has produced some of the most famous athletes in history.

The basic rules

  1. Only punches with closed gloves are allowed, and only above the waist on the front or side of the body and head.
  2. Win by knockout (KO): the opponent can't get up before the referee counts to 10.
  3. Win by technical knockout (TKO): the referee or doctor stops the fight because a fighter can't defend themselves.
  4. If the fight goes the distance, three judges score each round, usually 10–9 to the winner. Most rounds wins the decision.
  5. No hitting below the belt, holding and hitting, headbutts or hitting after the bell.
  6. There are four major title organizations (WBC, WBA, IBF, WBO). Holding all four belts makes you undisputed.

Famous names to know

  • Muhammad Ali“Float like a butterfly, sting like a bee.” Three-time heavyweight champion and cultural icon.
  • Mike TysonThe youngest heavyweight champion ever, famed for ferocious knockouts.
  • Floyd Mayweather Jr.Retired unbeaten at 50–0, a defensive genius.
  • Manny PacquiaoFilipino legend with world titles in eight weight divisions.
  • Canelo ÁlvarezMexican superstar of the 2010s and 2020s.
  • Claressa ShieldsAmerican, two-time Olympic gold medalist and undisputed pro champion.

Lingo you'll hear
