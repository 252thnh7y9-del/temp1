Surfing

Riding waves, judged on style, power and daring.

Competitors
Usually 2–4 surfers per heat
Heat length
About 20–35 minutes
Scoring
Best two waves count
Biggest events
The Olympics and the WSL Championship Tour

What is it?

In competitive surfing, athletes ride ocean waves and are scored by judges. Since every wave is different, surfers must choose the right ones and perform the most impressive maneuvers.

Surfing debuted at the 2020 Olympics. At Paris 2024 it was held 15,000 km away at Teahupo'o in Tahiti, one of the world's most dangerous waves.

The basic rules

  1. Five judges score each wave from 0 to 10, based on difficulty, innovation, variety, speed and power.
  2. Each surfer's best two waves are added for their heat score.
  3. Priority: the surfer with priority has the right to catch a wave first.
  4. Interfering with another surfer's wave brings a penalty.
  5. Heats are knockout rounds leading to a final.

Major leagues & competitions

  • World Surf League (WSL) Championship TourThe top pro circuit.
  • Olympic GamesMen's and women's shortboard.
  • Pipe MastersA legendary contest in Hawaii.

Famous breaks

  • Pipeline, HawaiiThe most famous wave in surfing.
  • Teahupo'o, TahitiA thick, dangerous wave, and the 2024 Olympic venue.
  • Jeffreys Bay, South AfricaA perfect right-hand point break.
  • Bells Beach, AustraliaThe oldest pro contest.

Famous names to know

  • Kelly SlaterAmerican with a record 11 world titles.
  • Duke KahanamokuHawaiian “father of modern surfing” and Olympic swimming champion.
  • Stephanie GilmoreAustralian eight-time world champion.
  • Carissa MooreAmerican, the first Olympic women's surfing champion.
  • Gabriel MedinaBrazilian three-time world champion.
