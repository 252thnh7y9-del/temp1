Luge

Feet-first on a tiny sled, decided by thousandths of a second.

Competitors
Singles, doubles and a team relay
Run length
About 40–55 seconds
Season
November to February
Olympic events
Men's and women's singles and doubles, team relay

What is it?

In luge, athletes lie on their backs on a small sled and slide feet-first down an icy track at up to 140 km/h (87 mph). They steer with tiny movements of their legs and shoulders.

It's the only Olympic sport timed to the thousandth of a second, because the margins are so small. Germany has dominated it for decades.

The basic rules

  1. In singles, athletes make 4 runs over two days. The lowest total time wins.
  2. The start: grab handles, rock back and forth, then launch and paddle the ice with spiked gloves.
  3. You must cross the finish on your sled. Falling off ends your chances.
  4. Weight limits apply, and lighter athletes may add weight.
  5. Women's doubles was added to the Olympics in 2026.
