Track & Field

Run fastest, jump highest, throw farthest. Sport at its simplest.

Competitors
Mostly individual, plus relays
Event length
From under 10 seconds (100 m) to over 2 hours (the marathon)
Season
Outdoor season roughly May to September
Biggest events
The Olympics and the World Athletics Championships

What is it?

Track and field, or “athletics” outside the US, is a collection of running, jumping and throwing events. It's the centerpiece of the Summer Olympics and one of the oldest sports in the world, going back to the ancient Olympic Games.

The appeal is simple: the result is a time or a distance. The 100-meter final decides the “world's fastest human,” and world records are celebrated moments.

The basic rules

  1. Sprints (100 m, 200 m, 400 m): stay in your lane. A false start means disqualification.
  2. Distance running covers the 800 m up to 10,000 m on the track, and the marathon (42.195 km / 26.2 mi) on roads.
  3. Jumps: long jump, triple jump, high jump and pole vault. Your best attempt counts.
  4. Throws: shot put, discus, javelin and hammer. Farthest legal throw wins.
  5. Relays: four runners pass a baton. Dropping it or passing outside the zone means disqualification.
  6. Decathlon (men, 10 events) and heptathlon (women, 7 events) test all-around athletes.

Major leagues & competitions

  • Olympic GamesThe pinnacle of the sport.
  • World Athletics ChampionshipsEvery two years.
  • Diamond LeagueA yearly series of elite meets.
  • World Marathon MajorsIncluding Boston, London, Berlin, Chicago, New York and Tokyo.
  • NCAA track and fieldA pipeline for many Olympic stars.

Powerhouse nations

  • United StatesThe most successful nation overall, especially in sprints.
  • JamaicaA sprinting superpower relative to its size.
  • Kenya & EthiopiaDominant in distance running.
  • Great BritainHistoric success across many events.

Famous names to know

  • Usain BoltJamaican with world records of 9.58 s (100 m) and 19.19 s (200 m).
  • Jesse OwensWon four golds at the 1936 Berlin Olympics.
  • Eliud KipchogeKenyan, the first to run a marathon distance in under 2 hours (2019).
  • Florence Griffith-Joyner“Flo-Jo,” whose 1988 sprint records still stand.
  • Mondo DuplantisSwedish pole vaulter who keeps raising his own world record.
  • Faith KipyegonKenyan middle-distance legend.

Lingo you'll hear

PB / PR
Personal best / personal record.
Anchor leg
The final runner in a relay.
Bell lap
The last lap of a distance race.
Kick
A final sprint at the end of a race.

💬 Sound smart at a party

“It's all about the start.” In the 100 m, reaction time can decide the race.
