Canoe & Kayak

Flat-out sprints and wild whitewater courses.

Competitors
Singles, doubles and fours
Sprint distances
200 m to 1000 m (500 m common)
Slalom
About 20 gates on a whitewater course
Biggest events
The Olympics and the World Championships

What is it?

Olympic paddling comes in two forms. In sprint, boats race in lanes on flat water. In slalom, paddlers navigate a whitewater course through hanging gates.

In a kayak, athletes sit and use a double-bladed paddle. In a canoe, they kneel and use a single-bladed paddle.

The basic rules

  1. Sprint: first across the line wins.
  2. Slalom: paddlers pass through each gate in order. Green gates go downstream and red gates upstream.
  3. Touching a slalom gate costs a 2-second penalty, and missing one costs 50 seconds.
  4. Kayak cross, which debuted in 2024, has four paddlers racing down the course together.
  5. Boats are named by type and crew size: K1 is a single kayak, C2 a two-person canoe.
